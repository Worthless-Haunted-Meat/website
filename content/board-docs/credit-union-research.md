# Choosing WHM's Credit Union

October 14, 2026

The October 2026 board consent lets the President choose a federally insured credit union for WHM's account and report the choice to the board. This is the research behind that choice, so the board can see how it was made and push back before the account is opened.

## Why not Chase

Chase was the first plan. Josh made the case for a local credit union instead, and it fits the mission better. WHM exists because the gains from new technology keep flowing to whoever already holds the capital. A publicly traded megabank is that pattern. A credit union is owned by its members, and its profits come back to them as lower fees and better rates.

## What we looked for

- Member-owned, not a publicly traded company.
- Open to a Texas nonprofit corporation based in Denton or Dallas County.
- No monthly fee and a small opening deposit. WHM has very little money yet.
- Two signers: Ricardo as primary and Josh as backup, with both approving anything over $1,000, as the consent requires.
- Shared branching (the CO-OP network) if possible, so a board member outside Texas can walk into a nearby credit union.
- Some sign that it cares about the same things we do.

## The plan

**First choice: Credit Union of Texas.** **Backup: Mobility Credit Union**, if Credit Union of Texas can't add a signer who lives outside Texas or if shared branching turns out to matter more.

## The shortlist

### 1. Credit Union of Texas (Allen; nearest branch in Lewisville)

- Member-owned. The federal credit union regulator (NCUA) designates it as serving mostly low-income members.
- It employs people with intellectual and developmental disabilities as branch greeters through Best Buddies. That's close to home for the NDNGenesis work.
- Most Texas residents can join, and it has an account built for nonprofits.
- Non-Profit Business Checking: $25 to open, no monthly fee, no minimum balance, 200 free transactions a month. Joining costs a one-time $20 fee and a $5 share deposit.
- Downsides: not on the shared branching network, and its website doesn't say whether a director can get view-only access.

### 2. Mobility Credit Union (Irving; nearest branch in Hurst)

- Member-owned. Anyone in Texas can join, and it has a nonprofit checking account.
- $100 to open, no monthly fee.
- On the shared branching network.
- Downside: less evidence that it shares our values. It has a diversity council and partners with the North Texas LGBT and Hispanic chambers.

### 3. Resource One Credit Union (Dallas)

- The strongest match on values. It is the only DFW credit union certified by the U.S. Treasury as a Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI), a lender to underserved communities. It is also minority-owned and serves mostly low-income members. Its partners include the Dallas Housing Coalition and Habitat for Humanity.
- Checking: $25 to open, no monthly fee.
- Why it isn't first: membership is limited to people who live or work in Dallas County, and WHM's only Dallas presence is a mailbox. It also ends shared branching on November 28, 2026. If WHM's work puts it in Dallas County, this is the one to come back to.

### 4. EECU (Fort Worth; nearest branch in Southlake)

- Member-owned. Open to organizations in Denton, Dallas and Tarrant counties, and on the shared branching network.
- Costs $10 a month unless the average balance is $1,000 or more.
- Search results (its site blocked a direct check) say every signer must be present to open the account. That's hard if a signer lives out of state.

### 5. Texans Credit Union (Richardson)

- Member-owned and on the shared branching network. It's the only one that clearly offers view-only online access.
- Its nonprofit checking costs $15 a month with no way to waive it, which is too much for us right now.

## Looked at and set aside

- **DATCU** (has a branch in Argyle): a business account needs an owner who has been a member for six months.
- **PointBank** (Argyle) and **Climate First Bank** (online, a certified B Corp): good options, but they're banks. The consent authorizes a credit union, so either would need a new consent.
- **Amalgamated Bank**: strong values and partly union-owned, but it's publicly traded.
- **Beneficial State Bank, Southern Bancorp, Sunrise Banks, HOPE Credit Union**: they only open these accounts in their own states.
- **Mercury and Relay** (app-based business banking): the money sits at partner banks we don't choose, and Mercury won't accept a mailbox as an address.

## What we'll ask before opening

1. Can a signer who lives outside Texas be added without visiting a branch?
2. Does each signer have to join the credit union personally?
3. Can the account require two approvals for payments over $1,000? If not, the consent already covers it: the second signer approves by email before the payment, and that approval is kept with WHM's records.
4. Will you accept Ricardo's home as WHM's physical location, with the Dallas mailbox for mail only? Federal rules require a physical location, and mailboxes are a common reason applications get stuck.
5. Can any director get view-only access to statements?

## What we'll bring

- The signed October 2026 board consent, with the Secretary's Certificate
- A Texas Comptroller Certificate of Account Status dated after October 12, 2026
- A Texas Secretary of State record showing WHM active and listing its officers
- The IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter and the EIN letter (CP 575)
