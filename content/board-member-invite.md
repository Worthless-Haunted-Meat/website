# Board Member Invitation — Worthless Haunted Meat

Subject: I'd like you to consider joining the board of Worthless Haunted Meat

I want to ask you something, and I want to be completely straight with you about what it means before you say yes.

I run a nonprofit called Worthless Haunted Meat. The name is intentional. It's a statement about what happens to people when the economy decides it's done with them — and a dare to do something about it.

Our mission: *We discover what people love — then we use technology to help them do more of it, and teach them to bring others along.*

That's it. We're not a charity in the traditional sense. We're a group of people who believe that rapidly developing technology — AI in particular — is the most powerful tool ever built, and that right now, almost all of that power is flowing toward people who already have everything. We want to change that. One person, one community, one skill set at a time. Teachers who love teaching but drown in paperwork. Developers who want to build things that matter. Communities that have been written off. We find what people love and we use technology to give them more of it — then we teach them to pass it on.

I'd like you on the board.

---

**Before you say yes, here's what you need to know.**

Serving on a nonprofit board comes with legal responsibilities. I'm not going to gloss over that.

As a board member you have three core duties under Texas law:

- **Duty of Care** — make reasonably informed decisions. Show up, read the materials, ask questions.
- **Duty of Loyalty** — put the organization's interests first. No self-dealing.
- **Duty of Obedience** — keep the organization true to its mission and legal obligations.

**What you are NOT responsible for:**

You are not personally responsible for the organization's debts. Worthless Haunted Meat is a registered Texas nonprofit corporation — that structure protects board members from the organization's financial obligations the same way an LLC protects its owners. If this nonprofit fails, creditors cannot come after your personal assets simply because you served on the board.

Texas also has strong volunteer protection laws — the Charitable Immunity and Liability Act — which shields nonprofit board members from personal liability for good-faith decisions made in service of the mission.

**Where real risk lives:**

The honest exceptions are narrow but worth knowing:

- If you personally guarantee a loan or contract on behalf of the organization, you are on the hook for that.
- If there are employees and payroll taxes go unpaid, the IRS can pursue board members personally. (We currently have zero employees and zero payroll.)
- Fraud or gross negligence — but if you're showing up and acting in good faith, this doesn't apply.

**Right now, practically speaking:**

Worthless Haunted Meat has zero revenue, zero employees, zero debt, and zero pending litigation. The practical risk of serving on this board today is as low as it gets. As the organization grows, we will put Directors and Officers (D&O) insurance in place to add another layer of protection.

I'd encourage you to consult your own attorney if you have any concerns. I'd rather you ask questions now than feel surprised later.
